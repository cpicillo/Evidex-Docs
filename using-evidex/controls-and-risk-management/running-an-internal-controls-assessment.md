# Running an Internal Controls Assessment

Running an internal controls assessment in Evidex allows organizations to **document, evaluate, and review the effectiveness of internal controls** related to privacy, security, and governance obligations.

Internal controls assessments provide structured inputs that can be used to:

* Identify control gaps or weaknesses
* Extract and document risks
* Support mitigation planning
* Demonstrate control design and implementation during audits

Control assessments do not create decisions on their own, but they form a critical input to risk identification and governance workflows.

### When to Run an Internal Controls Assessment

Organizations typically run internal control assessments when:

* Evaluating the design or effectiveness of existing controls
* Preparing for audits or certifications
* Responding to regulatory inquiries
* Assessing controls related to a new system or vendor
* Reviewing controls after a material change

Controls assessments may be repeated over time as controls evolve.

### How Internal Controls Assessments Work in Evidex

Internal controls assessments in Evidex are:

* Structured and template-driven
* Completed in draft before approval
* Linked to downstream risk workflows

Controls are documented intentionally and reviewed before being relied upon for decision-making.

### How to Run an Internal Controls Assessment

#### Step 1: Create a New Controls Assessment

From the Assessments area, select **Create Assessment** and choose the **Internal Controls Assessment** template.

This template is designed to capture:

* Control descriptions
* Control objectives
* Implementation details
* Evidence or supporting context

#### Step 2: Define the Scope

Clearly define the scope of the assessment, such as:

* Systems or processes covered
* Applicable frameworks or obligations
* Business units or vendors in scope

Clear scoping helps ensure controls are evaluated in the correct context.

<figure><img src="../../.gitbook/assets/Scope selection.png" alt=""><figcaption></figcaption></figure>

#### Step 3: Document Controls

For each control, document:

* What the control is designed to do
* How it is implemented
* Whether it is preventive, detective, or corrective
* Any known limitations or assumptions

Controls should be described factually and consistently.

* What the control is designed to do
* How it is implemented
* Whether it is preventive, detective, or corrective
* Any known limitations or assumptions

Controls should be described factually and consistently.

### What Evidex Records

During a controls assessment, Evidex records:

* Control descriptions and evaluations
* The user(s) who documented controls
* Version history and edits
* Timestamps for creation and updates

If AI assistance is used during drafting, corresponding AI audit log entries are created.
