# Extracting Risks from Controls

Extracting risks from controls allows organizations to **translate control analysis into clearly defined, reviewable risks**.

Rather than assuming that documented controls are sufficient, Evidex supports a structured process to:

* Identify gaps or weaknesses in controls
* Surface residual or contextual risks
* Create discrete risk items for review and decision-making

This ensures risks are **explicitly identified**, not inferred.

### When to Extract Risks from Controls

Organizations typically extract risks from controls when:

* An internal controls assessment has been completed
* Control gaps or limitations have been identified
* Controls rely on assumptions or external dependencies
* Controls mitigate risk partially rather than fully

Risk extraction may occur during initial assessment or as controls evolve over time.

### How Risk Extraction Works in Evidex

Risk extraction in Evidex is:

* **Human-initiated**
* **Context-driven**
* **Reviewable and auditable**

Evidex may assist with highlighting potential risks, but **risk creation is never automatic**.\
Each risk must be reviewed and confirmed by a user.

### How to Extract Risks from Controls

#### Step 1: Review Completed Controls

Begin by reviewing the internal controls assessment, focusing on:

* Controls marked as partially effective
* Identified weaknesses or limitations
* Dependencies on third parties, tooling, or processes
* Controls that mitigate but do not eliminate risk

#### Step 2: Identify Potential Risks

For each relevant control or control gap, identify:

* What could go wrong if the control fails or is insufficient
* The potential impact of that failure
* The context in which the risk would materialize

Risks should be described clearly and specifically.

#### Step 3: Create Risk Entries

From the controls assessment, create individual risk entries that:

* Reference the relevant control(s)
* Describe the risk condition
* Capture the source of the risk (e.g., control gap, dependency, limitation)

Each risk is created as a **distinct item**, not bundled or implied.

#### Step 4: Review and Refine Risk Descriptions

Before proceeding:

* Confirm the risk description is accurate and complete
* Avoid vague or overly broad language
* Ensure the risk reflects reality, not speculation

Clear risk descriptions support better decisions later.

### What Evidex Records

When risks are extracted from controls, Evidex records:

* The originating controls assessment
* The control(s) associated with the risk
* The user who created the risk
* Timestamps and version history
* Any AI-assisted analysis used during identification

These records support traceability from control to risk.

### Relationship to Decision-Making

Extracted risks:

* Must be reviewed
* Must have decision records created
* Must be approved before entering the risk register

Risk identification does not imply acceptance.

### Review Best Practices

To support defensible governance:

* Extract risks conservatively and clearly
* Avoid assuming controls eliminate all risk
* Separate operational issues from governance risks
* Document context and assumptions explicitly

This improves downstream approvals and audit review.
