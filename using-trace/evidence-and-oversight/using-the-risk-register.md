# Using the Risk Register

The Risk Register in TRACE provides a **central, authoritative view of all approved risks** and how they are being managed over time.

It serves as the system of record for:

* Identified risks
* Approved risk decisions
* Mitigation status
* Ongoing oversight and review

Rather than being a static list, the Risk Register reflects **decisions that have been formally reviewed and approved**.

### What Appears in the Risk Register

Only risks that have completed the appropriate governance steps appear in the Risk Register.

Specifically:

* Risks originate from approved assessments
* Each risk is associated with a documented decision
* Risk status reflects an approved outcome (e.g., accepted, mitigated, transferred, avoided)

Draft or unapproved risks do not appear in the Risk Register.

### How the Risk Register Is Used

Organizations typically use the Risk Register to:

* Review current risk posture
* Track mitigation progress
* Identify accepted or deferred risks
* Support management reporting
* Prepare for audits or regulatory reviews

The Risk Register is designed for **ongoing oversight**, not one-time reporting.

### Understanding Risk Status

Each risk in the register reflects its current, approved state.

Common statuses include:

* **Accepted** – Risk has been reviewed and explicitly accepted
* **Mitigated** – Mitigation measures have been defined and approved
* **Transferred** – Risk has been contractually or operationally transferred
* **Avoided** – Activity or processing has been altered to avoid the risk

Statuses are determined by decision records, not manual tagging.

### Reviewing Risks Over Time

The Risk Register supports longitudinal oversight by allowing users to:

* Monitor changes in risk status
* Review historical decisions
* Confirm mitigation completion
* Identify trends across assessments or business areas

Historical records remain available even as risk status evolves.

### What TRACE Records

For each risk in the register, TRACE preserves:

* Risk description and source
* Decision outcome and rationale
* Approval records
* Status changes over time
* Links to supporting assessments and evidence

These records are immutable and time-stamped.

### Role of the Risk Register in Audits

During audits or reviews, the Risk Register provides:

* A current snapshot of organizational risk posture
* Direct links to supporting decisions and approvals
* Evidence that risks are actively governed

This reduces reliance on spreadsheets or ad-hoc explanations.
